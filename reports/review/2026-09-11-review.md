### F-027 — A statistical review reports "you need 1,000,000 observations" where its own function refuses to name any number, and signs that figure into the artifact — **Medium**

**Claim.** `minimum_observations_for_significance` refuses, by design and with a
docstring explaining why, to return `_MAX_OBSERVATIONS` when no sample size would
establish the effect. `significance()` catches that refusal thirty lines later
and substitutes exactly the value the refusal exists to avoid, and
`significance_artifact` writes it into the signed content of a Validation Report.

**Evidence.** `framework/metrics/statistics.py:204-207` states the rule:

> An effect of exactly zero is refused rather than searched for. No sample size
> rescues a mean sitting on the null, and returning `_MAX_OBSERVATIONS` would
> read as "collect more data" when the honest answer is that there is nothing
> here to establish.

`framework/metrics/statistics.py:222-226` is that refusal. `:284-289` is the
substitution — `except ValueError: needed = _MAX_OBSERVATIONS` — and `:71` sets
`_MAX_OBSERVATIONS = 1_000_000`. `:302` writes it to `minimum_observations` and
`:303` derives `sample_adequate` from it. `:375` puts it in the artifact's
`parameters`, which `framework/metrics/reporting.py:219-232` places inside the
integrity hash.

Constructed and run against the repository as committed:

```
returns = [0.01, -0.01, 0.02, -0.02]      # mean exactly on a zero null

minimum_observations_for_significance(...)  ->  ValueError:
  "the observed mean is indistinguishable from the null, so no sample size
   makes it significant; this is not a shortage of data"

significance(...)                           ->  minimum_observations = 1000000
                                                sample_adequate      = False
                                                significant          = False, p = 1.0

significance_artifact(...).content includes:
  ('minimum_observations_for_significance', 1000000)
  ('sample_adequate', False)
```

The same substitution fires on a second, distinct case — an effect too small to
clear alpha within a million observations — so one number in the record stands
for three different states: "no effect exists", "no sample could ever show it",
and "a million would do it". No test constructs any of them: `grep -n
"minimum_observations\|sample_adequate" tests/metrics/*.py` returns eight hits,
all on series with a real non-zero effect, and `tests/metrics/test_significance.py:257`
covers only the zero-*dispersion* refusal, which is raised earlier by `p_value`
and never reaches this branch.

**Breach.** ADR-012 rule 8, `docs/DECISIONS.md:2231-2238` — "**A sample below
that number has not failed the test — it has not taken it**, and reporting those
two states identically is the failure this rule exists to prevent." The
substitution reports a conclusive result ("there is nothing here") in the words
of an inconclusive one ("the sample is too small"), which is that sentence's
prohibition run in the opposite direction. The constitutional line it lands on is
`constitution/Operational_Constraints.md:5` — "Belay shall maintain accurate
records."

**Failure.** *Fact for the behaviour, prediction for the cost.* Graded Medium and
not High deliberately: `significance_artifact` has no non-test caller today and
no Validation Report has ever been signed, so no false claim has yet been
produced. It becomes one on the first: Stage 3 gives this its first real caller,
a reviewer reads `minimum_observations_for_significance: 1000000` on a signed
artifact, and concludes that a strategy with no measurable edge is a data
problem — that more history would settle it. ADR-012 rule 8 exists precisely to
keep those two conclusions apart, and `constitution/Capital_Authority.md:11-14`
routes the result into position sizing. The record is append-only
(`Knowledge/Versioning.md:25`), so the figure cannot be corrected afterwards.

**Proposal.** Make the two states distinguishable in the result rather than
collapsing them. `minimum_observations` becomes `int | None`, `None` meaning "no
sample size applies", with the reason carried alongside — the exception already
carries it as a message. `sample_adequate` is then `False` for a short sample and
`None` for a question that does not arise. Roughly six lines in
`SignificanceResult` and `significance()`, plus a rendering choice in
`significance_artifact` so the artifact records the word rather than the number.
Two tests, each confirmed red first: the zero-effect series, and an effect too
small to clear `_MAX_OBSERVATIONS`.

### F-028 — CI states that the dashboard job fails when `scripts/status.py`'s regexes stop matching; it does not, and a one-token heading rewrite silently deletes roadmap stages while improving the completion ratio — **Medium**

**Claim.** `.github/workflows/conformance.yml` tells the reader its `dashboard
builds` job catches the exact failure mode `docs/DocumentStandard.md` exists to
prevent. Half that sentence is true. Three of the six regexes it refers to are
guarded by tests in `tests/test_status_dashboard.py`; the other three fall back
silently and the job stays green.

**Evidence.** `.github/workflows/conformance.yml:110-115`:

> Exits non-zero when the suite is not green, and fails outright if the regular
> expressions in `scripts/status.py` stop matching the prose they parse. That is
> not hypothetical … every one of them is a silent failure waiting for somebody
> to rewrite a sentence.

The first clause holds — `scripts/status.py:403` returns 1 when the suite is not
green. The second does not. No parse function raises; each returns an empty list
and the page renders "Nothing to report."

`scripts/status.py:182` matches a roadmap heading on a literal em dash:
`r"^## (Stage \d+ — .+?)(?:\s*\((.+)\))?$"`. Constructed against a scratch copy
— one heading, em dash replaced with a colon, nothing else changed:

```
$ sed -i 's/## Stage 3 — Backtester/## Stage 3: Backtester/' docs/ROADMAP.md
$ python -m pytest -q
632 passed, 1 skipped, 5 xfailed
$ python scripts/status.py
  roadmap stages      2 of 9 complete        # unmutated: 2 of 10
$ echo $?
0
```

The stage does not appear as missing or unparsed. It leaves the denominator, so
the dashboard's completion ratio **improves** from 20% to 22% and the progress
bar loses a segment — the failure runs in the flattering direction. `dashboard
builds` would be green.

Which of the six are covered, checked by `grep -rn` over `tests/`: only
`status.open_findings` and `status.PILLS` are referenced by any test —
four hits and one, all in `tests/test_status_dashboard.py`. `roadmap_stages`,
`decisions`, `untested_modules`, `module_health`, `run_tests`, `repo_stats` and
`implementation_state` are named by no test in the repository.

**Breach.** `constitution/Evidence_Standards.md:45` — "Every conclusion must
reference supporting evidence." Medium rather than High: the false half of the
sentence is a claim about a CI job, not about a strategy, and nothing computes on
it.

**This is narrower than it looks and narrower than an adjacent prior result.**
`reports/review/2026-09-04-review.md:678-684` lists the six regexes under "what
is not checkable today" and is correct that they are unchecked. The finding here
is the different one it did not make: a tracked document **states that they are
checked**, which is why nobody has written the tests. An unchecked surface that
is documented as unchecked costs nothing; one documented as covered is why it
stays uncovered.

**Failure.** *Fact for the mechanism, prediction for the cost.* A session
rewrites a ROADMAP heading — renaming Stage 3 when the Backtester is scoped is
the obvious occasion — and two numbers quietly become wrong in the direction that
flatters, on the one page `docs/HANDOFF.md:3480-3481` tells every session to
prefer to HANDOFF itself: "Run `refresh-status.cmd` first. The dashboard reports
current state more reliably than this document." CI reports green and the
workflow file says green means the regexes still match.

**Proposal.** Two lines and one test, and the test is the half that matters.
Delete the second clause of `.github/workflows/conformance.yml:110-112`, or make
it true. Making it true is cheap for `roadmap_stages()`: assert it returns at
least one stage and that every `^## Stage` line in `docs/ROADMAP.md` is matched
by it, the same shape `test_every_open_finding_has_a_parsed_priority` already
uses for the priority regex. Repeat for `decisions()` and `untested_modules()`.
Do not fix this by loosening the regex — a heading that does not match the
document standard is a fact worth surfacing, and the defect is that it is
surfaced as nothing.

**Claim.** An Accepted ADR assigns `departments/` to the governance layer and

### F-030 — `test_no_session_brief_is_orphaned` counts `docs/proposals/` as its own pointer set, so a ring of briefs nothing else references passes — **Low**

**Claim.** The test that exists to prove a session brief is reachable from a

**Evidence.** `tests/test_session_handoff.py:123-127` builds `pointers` from
`docs/HANDOFF.md`, `docs/OperatorChecklist.md` and `docs/OwnerDecisions.md` — and
then appends `"".join(read(p) for p in (REPO / "docs" / "proposals").glob("*.md"))`.
Its docstring at `:110-117` states the property it means to hold: "A brief nobody
links to is a brief the next session will not find … otherwise the brief is in
the same position as work on an unmerged branch, which is the failure that has
cost this repository twice."

Constructed against a scratch copy — two new briefs, each naming the other and
named by nothing outside the directory:

```
$ ls docs/proposals/
ADR-014-rule-5-amendment-DRAFT.md  F-007-session-brief.md
register-rebuild-session-brief.md  sibling-brief.md  unreachable-brief.md

$ grep -rl "unreachable-brief\|sibling-brief" --include="*.md" . | grep -v docs/proposals
(nothing)

$ python -m pytest -q tests/test_session_handoff.py

```

**Breach.** None in the constitution, so Low by the severity rule rather than by
judgement. The nearest governing sentence is `AGENTS.md:19` — "3. Review
docs/HANDOFF.md." — which puts that document, and not `docs/proposals/`, in every
session's mandatory reading. That is the distinction the pointer set erases.

**The two briefs on `main` are not orphaned in fact.** `docs/OperatorChecklist.md:24`
and `:26` name both `F-007-session-brief.md` and
`register-rebuild-session-brief.md`. The test is passing for the right reason
today. It would also pass for the wrong one, and the reason it exists at all —
its docstring records that it "caught one real orphan on its first run" — is that
this failure has already happened once.

**Failure.** *Prediction, and small.* A session writes a brief, points the
previous brief at it because that is the document in front of it, and closes.
The guard is green. The next session reads `docs/HANDOFF.md` and
`docs/OperatorChecklist.md`, as `AGENTS.md:19` and `:30` require, and never learns the
brief exists — the exact position of work on an unmerged branch, which is the
failure this repository has now paid for three times, twice in
`docs/OperatorChecklist.md:9-11`'s account and once more in F-026 above.

**Proposal.** Delete the second `"".join(...)` term at
`tests/test_session_handoff.py:127`, so `pointers` is the three documents a
session is required to read. Then add `reports/review/*.md` if a review report
should count as a pointer — that is a judgement, and it is the only one this
change forces. One line, and the test should be confirmed red against the two
briefs above before it is made green.
